## TL;DR

**Stage 2 is in. The WebGPU backend draws MToon's opaque, Mask,
transparent and outline pipelines and MMD's opaque, transparent and edge
pipelines on per-material bind groups, in the Vulkan backend's draw order,
with skinning, morphs and the late latch, at one sample or four. Seven new
parity scenes join the nine of stage 1, and all sixteen render at 1x and
4x over both clears. At 1x every new scene is identical to Vulkan through
Dawn's Direct3D 12 adapter, depth aside on two outline scenes, where the
hull's depth bias differs by at most 7.2e-7. At 4x every scene without a
hull or a Mask is within one step. Three things needed measuring: Slang
numbers WGSL locations by semantic index, not by declaration, so the
shared interfaces now carry their locations in their semantics;
NVIDIA's Vulkan alpha to coverage, which WebGPU's sample-mask Mask
reproduces, dithers over a 2 x 2 block, keeps samples in the order 3, 0,
1, 2 and needs fine derivatives, and with all three matched 256 coverage
shares agree on every pixel of the block; and an outline hull at 4x can
move a silhouette pixel by two steps, and its biased depth by 2.4e-6,
which become two measured classes of WGPU-Q1. Through Dawn's Vulkan
adapter every scene is identical but for one Mask pixel on a coverage
threshold. The RGBA output, morph and late-latch GPU checks, and the
headless run's nineteen material, outline, MSAA and skinning checks, now
run on WebGPU as well and pass. Vulkan's output is byte-identical.**

## 1. Shader interfaces

Generating the MToon and MMD modules (report 90 left MMD's surfaces
failing Tint) showed how `slangc -target wgsl` assigns locations: a
varying or vertex input whose semantic has a nonzero index takes that index
as its location, and the rest fill the free locations in declaration order.
SPIR-V generation ignores semantics and numbers by declaration. MToon's
stages agreed only because `NORMAL`, `TEXCOORD1` and `TEXCOORD2` happen to
land as their declarations do; MMD's edge input put the edge scale at
location 1 and the normal at 2, against the pipelines' streams; and a
struct with semantics used as an ordinary function's parameter or result,
as `MmdSurfaceVertex` used `VertexInput` and `VertexStageOutput`, got
`@location(0)` on every member, which Tint rejects. `[[vk::location]]` is
ignored by the WGSL target.

So every shared stage interface now names its place in its semantic:
streams `POSITION`, `NORMAL1`, `TEXCOORD2`, a fourth at index 3; varyings
`NORMAL`, `TEXCOORD1`, `TEXCOORD2`, `TEXCOORD3`. `SurfaceVertex` takes and
returns plain values, and the unused `VertexInput` went with it. Each of
the 22 Vulkan entry points' SPIR-V was compiled before and after: twenty
are byte-identical; MMD's two surface vertex stages differ in their ids and
function layout, from the restructured helper. Rendering all sixteen
parity scenes at 1x and 4x over both clears with the old and the new MMD
vertex SPIR-V gave 64 byte-identical Vulkan images.

Two more WGSL quirks: an `SV_Depth` result is written as `@location(0)`,
while an `SV_Depth` struct member becomes `@builtin(frag_depth)`, so the
depth resolve returns a struct; and Slang has no WGSL spelling of
fine derivatives, which `__intrinsic_asm` supplies (§3).

The WebGPU build now also compiles `mtoon`, `mtoon_transparent`,
`mtoon_outline`, `mmd`, `mmd_transparent`, `mmd_outline` and the backend's
own `webgpu/mtoon_coverage` and `webgpu/depth_resolve`, all accepted by
Tint with uniformity analysis and no relaxation.

## 2. The backend

- **Pipelines.** `ScenePipelines` gains MToon, MToonCoverage,
  MToonTransparent, MToonOutline, Mmd, MmdTransparent and MmdOutline,
  created per cull, front face, depth write and stride combination as draws
  need them, with Vulkan's blending, MMD's `LessEqual` depth test and the
  hulls' depth bias (constant 1, slope 1) as pipeline state. The surface
  kinds share one layout and four streams; the fourth is the tangent frame,
  MMD's sub-texture UV or its edge scale, a zero or unit element bound
  with stride 0 when the mesh has none.
- **Bind groups.** Group 1 binds all three model buffers (MToon at 0, MMD
  at 1, PreviewSurface at 2) and nine role textures and samplers, visible
  to the vertex stage too, for `mtoon_outline`'s width texture. The vertex
  stage then has exactly WebGPU's eight storage buffers: the frame, two
  models and five deformation buffers; the shadow views' parameters and
  MMD's new shadow parameters (group 0, binding 4) are fragment-only.
  MMD's shared toon ramps are the texture cache's, and its toon role reads
  through the clamp sampler. MMD's shadow parameters carry
  `mmd_morph_tints`; no map is drawn on this backend yet.
- **Materials.** The MToon and MMD slots are written by the shared
  `WriteParameters`; an MMD material's transparency follows its textures'
  alpha every frame, and the outline width texture's zero-green test skips
  a hull, as on Vulkan. Outline frustum and occlusion culling use the
  core's `OutlineBounds` and `OutlineOcclusion`.
- **Order.** `MeshCache::Record` follows the Vulkan backend's: unlit, opaque
  MToon hulls, opaque surfaces, transparent draws by queue (an MMD mesh's
  in material order, a double-sided MToon surface's back then front, then
  its hull), then MMD's edges. The draw ring holds three draws per part.
- **4x.** A request of four or more renders at four, anything less at
  one. Colour resolves as the pass ends; depth, which WebGPU does not
  resolve, is resolved by `webgpu/depth_resolve`, a pass writing each
  pixel's sample 0, as Vulkan's sample-zero resolve.

The stage 1 refusal of MToon and MMD draw lists is gone.

## 3. Mask coverage at 4x

`webgpu/mtoon_coverage` is mtoon_opaque's fragment writing
`@builtin(sample_mask)` with alpha 1. Its first form kept the nearest whole
number of samples, the lowest by index, and the 4x MToon Mask scene
differed by up to 128 steps with 448 pixels of depth coverage apart. The
parity test gained a coverage probe: a quad of constant alpha, whose
`fwidth` is 0, so `MaskCoverage` gives an exact share, read per pixel on
both backends. It showed what NVIDIA's Vulkan driver does:

- **Count.** In 255ths, a pixel keeps one sample from 7.5, and one more
  every 64 after (sweeping 1,025 shares: transitions at 7.6, 71.7, 135.7
  and 199.7, within the probe's 0.25 resolution).
- **Dither.** The thresholds move by 0, 32, 48 and 16 for the pixels
  (even, even), (odd, even), (even, odd) and (odd, odd) of each 2 x 2 block.
- **Order.** Quads covering half a pixel showed the kept samples nest as 3,
  then 0, 1 and 2 of the standard 4x positions: one kept sample is not
  sample 0, so the depth product shows no coverage there.
- **Derivatives.** With those three, the scene's remaining differences sat
  only on the second, third and fourth pixel of each 2 x 2 quad, where
  coarse and fine derivatives differ. Dawn's Direct3D 12 device takes WGSL's
  plain `fwidth` coarse; with `fwidthFine` the scene agreed.

The shader now does all four. Every explicit derivative in the shared
modules (`MaskCoverage`'s width, the derivative tangent frame, the shadow
receivers' plane, MMD's self-shadow plane) goes through the bindings'
`DerivativeX`, `DerivativeY` and `DerivativeWidth`: macros for plain
`ddx`, `ddy` and `fwidth` on Vulkan, so its SPIR-V is unchanged, and
`dpdxFine`, `dpdyFine` and `fwidthFine` on WebGPU. Beyond the Mask edge
they changed one channel of one pixel of the measured scenes.

The probe is now part of `toon-webgpu-parity`: 256 shares in whole 255ths,
each half a step from every threshold, over the four pixels of a dither
block, must keep as many samples and cover sample 0 alike. 768 of the 1,024
readings are partial, and none differs, through either adapter. The
dither, order and count are this driver's; another vendor's alpha to
coverage is its own, and a parity run there measures it again.

## 4. Parity

`toon-webgpu-parity` renders each scene through a fresh Vulkan and WebGPU
renderer at 128 x 128, shared shadows off, over the opaque and transparent
clears, at one sample and at four. The new scenes: a lit MToon sphere with
rim and a world-width outline under a directional key; MToon with base,
shade, MatCap and normal textures and a screen-width outline; MToon Mask
over a checker with alpha holes; MToon Blend, double-sided, with an outline,
over an opaque MToon sphere; MMD with a shared toon ramp, specular and an
edge scaled per vertex; MMD with additive and multiplying spheres, one
diffuse-transparent with a translucent edge; and a two-joint skinned MToon
sphere with a morph and an outline.

Largest channel difference, pixels more than one step apart (of 16,384)
and largest depth difference, the worse of the two clears:

| Scene | 1x, Direct3D 12 | 4x, Direct3D 12 | 4x, Dawn's Vulkan adapter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| stage 1's nine | as report 91 | ≤ 1, 0 | 0, 0 |
| mtoon-lit-outline | 0, 0, 5.4e-7 | 1, 0, 2.4e-6 | 0, 0 |
| mtoon-textured | 0, 0 | 2, 1 | 0, 0 |
| mtoon-mask | 0, 0 | 64, 1 | 64, 1 |
| mtoon-blend-outline | 0, 0 | 0, 0 | 0, 0 |
| mmd-shared-toon-edge | 0, 0 | 0, 0 | 0, 0 |
| mmd-sphere-transparent | 0, 0 | 1, 0 | 0, 0 |
| mtoon-skinned-morphed | 0, 0, 7.2e-7 | 1, 0, 8.3e-7 | 0, 0 |

Depth is otherwise identical, coverage identical in every row, and neither
backend raised a validation message. Through Dawn's Vulkan adapter, 1x is
identical everywhere.

Three rows needed explaining:

- **Hull depth.** Zeroing the bias's slope factor on both backends brought
  mtoon-lit-outline's depth difference from 2.4e-6 to 1.2e-7. Vulkan lets
  a driver take the slope as the larger screen slope or their length;
  Direct3D 12 takes the larger. Scenes with hulls get a depth limit of
  5e-6.
- **Hull edges.** With its outline off, mtoon-textured is identical at 4x;
  with the slope factor zeroed it is not, so its two-step pixel is a sample
  at the edge of a thin hull falling on the other side when the two
  compilers place the hull's vertices a last bit apart, a quarter of the
  hull's difference from its surface. Hull scenes at 4x get at most two
  steps on at most 2e-4 of their pixels.
- **Mask thresholds.** mtoon-mask's one pixel keeps one sample more on
  both adapters: its share sits on a threshold, which a last-bit
  difference in alpha or its derivative crosses. MToon Mask at 4x gets at
  most one sample (64 steps) on at most 2e-4 of its pixels, coverage
  identical.

These are WGPU-Q1's multisampled classes; every other scene keeps stage
1's limits at 4x.

## 5. GPU checks on WebGPU

The RGBA output, morph and late-frame GPU tests took a backend factory, as
the PreviewSurface test did in stage 1, and run on WebGPU as
`toon-rgba-output-webgpu`, `toon-morph-webgpu` and
`toon-late-frame-webgpu`; `toon-preview-surface-webgpu` now runs at 4x too.
All pass unchanged:

- RGBA output at 1x and 4x: both clears, the three models' constant
  shading and premultiplied silhouettes against independent values, Blend
  layering, MToon Mask at its cutoff keeping half its samples with alpha
  not applied twice, and MToon and MMD hulls alone.
- Morphs, with and without a skin, unlit, MToon opaque with an outline,
  Blend and Mask: 36 frames each against CPU-deformed rest geometry on the
  same backend, colour exact and depth within 1e-6, with only weight writes
  per frame.
- The late latch at 4x: 18 frames whose pose, morph weight, material and
  camera come from the late-frame source match a renderer given those
  values directly, exactly, with one pose, weight and material write per
  frame and nothing structural; a structural late sample is rejected and
  the extracted frame drawn.

The headless evidence run's checks took a renderer factory too, and
`toon-headless --backend webgpu` now runs the nineteen that stage 1 skipped
for stage 2: MToon's opaque, textured, rest, normal, rim and transparent
checks, scene lights, the outline's sampling, width, motion, frustum,
depth-clip and occlusion checks, MMD's surface, morph and edge checks, MSAA
and GPU skinning. All pass through both of Dawn's adapters, 26 checks in
all, with the five shadow checks skipped for stage 3. Two checks assert
that edits create no pipeline, which the Vulkan backend's pipelines,
created with the renderer, keep from the first frame. The WebGPU backend
creates each state combination when a draw first needs it (policy §4), so
on it the checks require that a pose or render queue edit creates none and
that a repeated frame creates none. The Vulkan run still passes its 31.
