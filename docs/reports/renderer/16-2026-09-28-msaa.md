# Anti-aliasing: 4x MSAA with alpha to coverage for Mask, and the avatar's silhouettes, hull edges and hair resolve

The viewport baseline quality and repeated motion-cost evaluation follows
in [report 27](27-2026-10-01-antialiasing-quality.md).

- Date: 2026-09-28
- Machine: Windows 11, MSVC 14.51, NVIDIA RTX A5000
- Tooling: `ost 0.23.13`; the canonical runtime
  `openstrata-runtime-cy2026-lookdev:26.08-gl-windows-x86_64`
  (`sha256:b982656c…`) and the published `vrmImaging` 0.10.0
  (`sha256:894fd616…`) of [report 12](12-2026-09-28-published-vrmimaging.md)
- Occasion: the first item of the
  [roadmap's priority](../../roadmap/README.md#priority), the anti-aliasing
  approach, in [v0.2.0](../../releases/v0.2.0.md)

## TL;DR

**The approach is MSAA: 4 samples per pixel by default, resolved as the
scene pass ends, with a Mask material's cut turned into coverage by alpha
to coverage.** Not TAA, which ghosts under avatar motion and works against
late motion latching, and not FXAA or SMAA, which cannot recover a hull or
strand thinner than a pixel ([design policy §16](../../design/DESIGN_POLICY.md#16-render-pipeline)).
On the avatar, 4x changes 8,331 pixels against 1x, 2,636 of them by more
than 24 levels, all on edges: the silhouette, the hair's strands, the eyes,
the ribbon and the hull's edge, including the dark specks where the hull
shows through at 1x. At 1 sample the renderer gives
report 15's image back exactly, pixel for pixel. `toon:msaaSamples` sets the
count in a Hydra host.

## 1. What changed

- **Backend.** `RenderOptions::samples`, 4 by default, is given to
  `CreateOffscreenRenderer` and `CreatePresentSession`. The renderer takes
  the device's highest count at or below it that its colour and depth
  formats offer together as transient attachments, and records it as
  `OffscreenStatistics::samples` (`PresentStatistics::samples`). Every
  scene pipeline rasterizes at that count. Multisampled, the scene pass
  draws into transient sample targets, allocated with the products and
  reallocated only with them, and resolves them with dynamic rendering's
  resolve attachments as it ends: colour by averaging into the colour
  product, or into the swapchain image, and depth by taking sample 0 into
  the depth product. The samples are not stored. A resolve is a colour
  attachment write at the colour output stage, depth's included, as the
  Vulkan specification's multisample resolve operations state, and the
  depth product's barriers name that stage and access when it is resolved.
- **Mask.** `mtoon_opaque` enables alpha to coverage when multisampled, and
  its fragment returns its coverage as alpha: 1 for Opaque; for Mask, with
  the draw flag `kDrawAlphaToCoverage`, `(alpha − cutoff) / fwidth(alpha) +
  0.5`, saturated, so the cut spreads over the pixel the alpha takes to
  cross the cutoff; a fragment with none is discarded. Without the flag, at
  one sample, Mask keeps or cuts a fragment whole, as before.
  `mtoon_outline` blends by its surface's alpha, and coverage from the same
  alpha would apply it twice, so a hull still cuts a Mask fragment whole.
- **`hdToon`.** The render setting `toon:msaaSamples`, 4 by default, is a
  descriptor of the delegate. A frame that finds another count than its
  renderer was made for destroys the renderer and creates one for the new
  count, which uploads the scene again: a settings change, not a per-frame
  one. The frame evidence adds `samples`.
- **`toon-viewport`.** `--samples N`, and the sample count is printed with
  the device.

## 4. Observations

- **The edges are what changes.** Mapped, the pixels 4x changes by more
  than 24 levels outline the avatar and its parts: the hair's outer strands
  and tips, the bangs' strands over the face, the eyes and eyelashes, the
  ribbon, the arms, the skirt's frills and the legs. The smaller changes,
  24 levels or less, are scattered through the surfaces along their
  triangle edges, where a pixel's samples are split between two triangles,
  each shading it once at the pixel's centre. Shading is still sampled once
  per triangle per pixel.
- **The hull's specks go.** At 1x the ribbon's rim and the hair beside the
  face carry single dark pixels, which look like the hull's edge winning a
  pixel from the surface in front of it; at 4x they resolve into the edge. That is the
  outline aliasing [v0.2.0](../../releases/v0.2.0.md)'s outline item names;
  its flicker under animation is still that item's to measure.
- **8x adds little.** Against 4x it moves 574 pixels by more than 24
  levels, most by a few levels: 4 is the default, which every Vulkan
  device offers for colour and depth attachments.
- **1x is the renderer as it was.** Its image equals report 15's exactly:
  an Opaque fragment returns alpha 1, as before, and without alpha to
  coverage Mask keeps or cuts whole fragments.
- **A count is a renderer.** Setting `toon:msaaSamples` rebuilt the
  renderer on the next frame, which uploaded the avatar again, 20 meshes,
  20 skins and 7 textures, and drew the same draws.
- **Memory.** Multisampled, the targets add a colour and a depth sample per
  sample per pixel: 32 bytes a pixel at 4x, 10.3 MB at this AOV and 66 MB
  at 1920×1080, twice that at 8x. They are transient attachments; on this
  desktop device they are ordinary device-local memory.

## 5. Not checked

Performance: no GPU timing exists yet (the renderer's telemetry is
[v0.3.0](../../roadmap/v0.3.0.md)'s), so the cost of the samples and the
resolve was not measured. A Mask material on a real asset: the avatar has
none, so alpha to coverage is checked by the headless scene alone.
Shading aliasing, which MSAA does not touch: specular-like rim and MatCap
edges, and the toony terminator under motion. Flicker under animation.
Attributes are interpolated at the pixel's centre, not its centroid, so a
partly covered pixel at a UV seam may sample across the seam; no fringe
was looked for.
Other vendors, Linux, and tile-based devices, where transient attachments
would be lazily allocated. Synchronization validation: the validation
layer ran with its default checks.
