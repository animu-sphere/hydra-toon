# v0.2.0 — MToon quality and the viewport foundation

v0.1.0 made MToon draw. v0.2.0 takes it from drawing to something a VRM
avatar can be trusted to look right in, and builds the dedicated viewport
that judges it, as the base of renderer work from here on
([design policy §31](../design/DESIGN_POLICY.md#31-evaluation-hosts)). It
brings what v0.1.0 established to practical quality, rather than adding new
breadth.

**Done when** a representative VRM avatar displays stably at practical
quality — Opaque, Mask and Blend materials, the remaining MToon texture
roles, an outline that stays stable under animation, basic scene lights and a
settled anti-aliasing baseline — and the dedicated viewport shows, times and
compares it, on Windows.

Linux, AMD and Intel Vulkan are not part of this milestone or its release
gate; they are [platform and GPU coverage](later.md#platform-and-gpu-coverage),
taken up after the renderer is settled.

The sections are in the order the work is taken.

## Viewport foundation

`toon-viewport` becomes the renderer's main evaluation host:

- a representative avatar in front of it: a build intent enabling the Hydra
  adapter and the viewport together, `--usd <stage>` hosting Hydra in the
  viewport's own frame loop, and the image presented by a GPU copy with no
  CPU readback, as `hydra-merlin`'s viewport does
  ([design policy §31](../design/DESIGN_POLICY.md#31-evaluation-hosts));
  with `vrmImaging` in the run, its MToon selection measured
  ([§31.1](../design/DESIGN_POLICY.md#311-open-questions), DP-Q1);
- an orbit, pan and zoom camera, framing, and a camera reset;
- the MSAA sample count switched while the viewport runs;
- frame time, CPU and GPU timing, and renderer statistics on screen;
- basic material and light debug controls;
- screenshot and image capture;
- the automated presentation test (`--hidden --frames N`) drawing the
  `usdview` smoke stage through Hydra as well as the built-in scene, and
  asserting that a normal frame reads nothing back.

## Outline stability and cost

The inverted hull is kept and finished before any other method
([design policy §10](../design/DESIGN_POLICY.md#10-outline)):

- stable width in screen and world units;
- aliasing of thin outlines, flicker under animation, and the silhouette's
  temporal stability;
- depth conflict between the hull and its surface under animation;
- how the width texture is sampled;
- no hull drawn that cannot be seen, and the draw and GPU cost measured in
  the viewport, as a report.

## The rest of MToon

Enough of MToon 1.0 that representative VRM 1.0 avatars draw naturally; not
every feature has to be complete at once:

- UV animation: scroll and rotation by time, masked by `uvAnimationMask`;
- the emissive texture, the shading shift texture and its scale, and the
  normal map, with authored normals read where a mesh has them;
- the texture roles `hdToon` does not read yet, and correct texture
  transforms for every role;
- mask and culling correctness across the avatar's materials.

## Scene lights

From the fixed stand-in light to Hydra's lights
([design policy §32](../design/DESIGN_POLICY.md#32-scene-lighting)):

```text
HdLight / scene index → ToonLight → light buffer → MToon
```

- directional lights, basic point and spot lights, and the ambient
  contribution, with MToon's lighting semantics over them;
- basic light controls in the viewport.

Clustered lighting, a production shadow system and complex image-based
lighting are not part of this milestone.

## Anti-aliasing quality

MSAA evaluated as the baseline in the viewport — on the outline and on thin
features: hair strands, eyelashes, facial lines — and the standard it sets
recorded as a report. FXAA or TAA is considered only if that evaluation asks
for it ([design policy §16](../design/DESIGN_POLICY.md#16-render-pipeline)).

## VRM reproduction reports

Representative VRM avatars drawn in the viewport and in `usdview`, and
compared with a reference MToon renderer such as UniVRM or three-vrm, each a
[report](../reports/).
