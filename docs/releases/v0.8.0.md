# v0.8.0 — Host integration and GPU handoff

**Release status:** prepared on 2026-10-25. Tag `v0.8.0` follows a green
[release workflow dry run](README.md#how-a-release-is-cut) on the prepared
`main`. The tagged workflow creates a draft; a human publishes it.

## What this milestone establishes

The reusable RGBA product reaches a host application on the GPU, without
per-frame CPU readback, under the
[GPU handoff policy](../design/GPU_HANDOFF_POLICY.md), which realizes the
logical contract of
[render output policy §7](../design/RENDER_OUTPUT_POLICY.md#7-gpu-texture-handoff)
for one integration: an in-process Vulkan image handoff on a device the host
owns. Readback stays as the explicit, measured fallback.

- **A lent device.** `<toon/vulkan_handoff.hpp>` publishes the device
  requirements (`VulkanHandoffRequirements`) and creates the offscreen
  renderer on a device the host lends (`CreateOffscreenRenderer(LentVulkanDevice, …)`),
  refusing a device that lacks a requirement with the item named. It is the
  same renderer an owned device gets, and it never destroys the device.
- **The handoff.** `CreateHandoffRenderer` returns a `HandoffRenderer`
  whose `RenderHandoff` draws into a slot of a ring of two to four slots,
  three by default
  ([HOST-Q1](../design/GPU_HANDOFF_POLICY.md#11-open-questions), resolved),
  and hands it off in `SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL` with the policy's
  descriptor. Consumers `Acquire` and `Release` frames with their own
  timeline semaphores, which the slot's next use waits for on the GPU. Held
  slots, resize generations, several consumers, capture and a `DeviceLost`
  status follow policy §7.
- **The reference consumer.** `toon-handoff-gpu` samples handed-off frames
  with the composite shaders and compares them byte for byte with the CPU
  composite of the readback product; `toon-handoff-measure`, built with the
  Hydra adapter, measures the handoff beside readback (policy §8).
- **The Tessera host.** The opt-in `tessera-usd` intent fetches Tessera at
  a pinned commit and builds `toon-tessera-host`, which draws an avatar
  through the handoff and a Tessera panel into one swapchain image, binding
  each slot as a premultiplied Tessera image
  ([HOST-Q2, HOST-Q3](../design/GPU_HANDOFF_POLICY.md#11-open-questions),
  resolved; Tessera's per-image `ImageAlpha` landed in its own repository).
- **Readback, faster.** The offscreen readback buffers prefer host-cached
  memory: a small scene's 1,920 × 1,080 render call fell from 73 ms to
  1.6 ms, which `hdToon` shares. `OffscreenStatistics` reports GPU frame
  time where the queue writes timestamps, the frame it belongs to, and the
  bytes read back.

Stages 1 to 3 are renderer reports
[97](../reports/renderer/97-2026-10-25-handoff-stage-1.md),
[98](../reports/renderer/98-2026-10-25-handoff-stage-2.md) and
[99](../reports/renderer/99-2026-10-25-handoff-stage-3.md). The
[capability matrix](../reference/CAPABILITY_MATRIX.md) owns current status.

## Acceptance

[Renderer report 100](../reports/renderer/100-2026-10-25-handoff-acceptance.md)
meets the milestone's done-when condition on one machine, every piece of the
policy's [evidence](../design/GPU_HANDOFF_POLICY.md#10-evidence) rerun from
one commit:

- The reference consumer equals the CPU composite of the readback product
  byte for byte for both RGBA fixtures at 1x and 4x, through capture, two
  consumers of one frame, full rings of two, three and four slots, a GPU
  wait for an unreached release, a resize and a recreated device, with
  validation and synchronization validation silent.
- The Tessera host's presented frames, at 1x and 4x, through a resize and
  both paths, are within one sRGB step of the composite of the product the
  consumers sample, 99.2–99.8 % of pixels exactly, and were inspected.
- Beside readback, over 300 frames, the handoff reads nothing back and
  removes the CPU wait: AliciaSolid at 3,840 × 2,160 is ready 0.91 ms after
  the call starts instead of 6.36 ms, and the GPU frame falls rather than
  rises. The Tessera host's frame is 3.4–5.5 times shorter on the small
  stages.

The release gate's local runs are
[report 101](../reports/renderer/101-2026-10-25-v080-preparation.md).

## Compatibility and artifacts

- Windows x86_64, Vulkan 1.3 and canonical CY2026 OpenUSD 26.08 `lookdev`.
  A lending host's device must meet `VulkanHandoffRequirements()`: Vulkan
  1.3, a graphics queue, four features and the colour, depth and shadow
  formats, with no device extension. Measured configuration in
  [supported configurations](../reference/SUPPORTED_CONFIGURATIONS.md).
- `vulkan_handoff.hpp` is a new installed public header; existing
  renderers, `hdToon` and the viewport keep their own paths (policy §9),
  and their output is unchanged. `OffscreenStatistics` gains fields.
- `Toon::AvatarState` builds against experimental runtime ABI revision 4
  or 5; a revision-5 build refuses a nonempty primary UV output.
- Nothing installed links Tessera; `toon-tessera-host` is built from source
  with the `tessera-usd` intent and is never installed.
- The release lane produces the `hydra` binary package, manifest, SPDX SBOM,
  source archive, checksums and archive/OCI pins. No published digest is
  claimed before the tagged workflow. The published package still needs the
  Formation GPU check described in the [release procedure](README.md).

## Known limitations

- One integration: in-process, the same `VkDevice` and queue family, Vulkan
  only, on Windows, one machine and GPU, NVIDIA RTX A5000. A WebGPU
  handoff, cross-process and cross-API sharing (Spout, Direct3D 11 shared
  handles), Hgi interop and a zero-copy `hdToon` AOV, other AOVs, other
  devices and Linux are outside the policy
  ([GPU handoff policy §12](../design/GPU_HANDOFF_POLICY.md#12-outside-this-policy));
  the WebGPU backend keeps readback.
- Device loss cannot be forced; recovery is shown by a destroyed and
  recreated device.
- The Tessera host is an example at scale 1, comparable with the
  reference consumer within one sRGB step, not byte for byte.
- A burst of about 0.15 s a few seconds into each process slows the CPU and
  GPU together on the kitchen; its cause is not identified, and kitchen
  measurements warm up for 400 frames to keep it out of the series
  ([report 100 §3](../reports/renderer/100-2026-10-25-handoff-acceptance.md#3-the-handoff-beside-readback)).
  Display latency remains the separate measurement task in the
  [later roadmap](../roadmap/later.md#not-yet-in-a-milestone).
- Material, shadow, output and backend limits are those of
  [v0.7.0](v0.7.0.md#known-limitations) and the releases it names.
