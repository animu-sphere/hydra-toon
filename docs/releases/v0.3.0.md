# v0.3.0 — avatar animation fast path

**Release status:** prepared on 2026-10-11. Tag `v0.3.0` follows a green
[release workflow dry run](README.md#how-a-release-is-cut) on `main`.
The tagged workflow creates a draft; a human publishes it.

## What this milestone establishes

A continuously changing avatar is the renderer's first-class workload. Pose,
morph, expression, look-at, material and camera changes reach the GPU on a
fast path: no scene or pipeline rebuild and no full geometry upload. Results
from `usd-avatar-runtime`'s real providers arrive on that path. They equal
the same values composed by UsdImaging and usdSkelImaging, and their
generated-source ages are measured to present API return.

- **GPU morphs.** Sparse targets before skinning in every scene pipeline,
  with Hydra normal offsets and inbetweens. A weight change writes only the
  weight buffer. Renderer reports
  [34](../reports/renderer/34-2026-10-04-gpu-morphs.md) and
  [35](../reports/renderer/35-2026-10-05-morph-normals.md).
- **Expression and late input.** Transient morph-weight and material
  overrides need no USD authoring or Hydra sync. Both Vulkan sessions read
  evaluated poses, weights, materials and camera after the GPU and acquire
  waits, and reject structural candidates atomically. Reports
  [36](../reports/renderer/36-2026-10-05-expression-overrides.md) and
  [39](../reports/renderer/39-2026-10-05-late-frame-input.md).
- **The viewport as the evaluation host.** Wall-clock playback; Morphs,
  Materials and Skeleton panels; latency and upload telemetry. Reports
  [37](../reports/renderer/37-2026-10-05-viewport-playback.md),
  [38](../reports/renderer/38-2026-10-05-viewport-morph-debug.md),
  [40](../reports/renderer/40-2026-10-05-viewport-skeleton-debug.md) and
  [41](../reports/renderer/41-2026-10-05-viewport-material-debug.md).
- **The evaluated-state consumer.** The optional `Toon::AvatarState`
  adapter takes the runtime's experimental C ABI revision 4. The Hydra
  delegate describes its resident targets, and the host-side matcher binds
  runtime identities to them. Reports
  [42](../reports/renderer/42-2026-10-05-avatar-state-adapter.md) to
  [46](../reports/renderer/46-2026-10-10-avatar-target-matching.md) and
  [48](../reports/renderer/48-2026-10-11-avatar-emissive-factor.md).
- **Real providers and parity.** The runtime's VRM expression/LookAt
  provider and its motion clip provider ran on a committed fixture, on two
  local avatars and with seven VRMA clips. Each result's fast output equals
  the session-layer oracle in palettes, subshape weights, materials, draw
  packets and pipeline selection, and colour/depth within declared
  tolerances. A replaced scene is refused until it is rebound. Reports
  [47](../reports/renderer/47-2026-10-11-avatar-real-providers.md),
  [50](../reports/renderer/50-2026-10-11-avatar-motion-provider.md) and
  [51](../reports/renderer/51-2026-10-11-v030-evidence.md).
- **The dedicated viewport on the runtime.** `toon-viewport-avatar` applies
  results that arrive after extraction at the late read. It runs through
  instance resets and scene replacement with no static upload and no extra
  Hydra sync, and shows each runtime frame in a diagnostics panel.
  Generated expression and gaze input is aged at the late read, buffer
  write, submit and present API return. Reports
  [49](../reports/renderer/49-2026-10-11-viewport-avatar-runtime.md) and
  [51](../reports/renderer/51-2026-10-11-v030-evidence.md).

## Compatibility and artifacts

**Platform.** Windows x86_64, a Vulkan 1.3 device and OpenStrata's canonical
CY2026 OpenUSD 26.08 `lookdev` runtime. VRM material imaging needs
`vrmImaging` 0.10.0, as in v0.2.0. Measured configurations are in
[SUPPORTED_CONFIGURATIONS.md](../reference/SUPPORTED_CONFIGURATIONS.md).

**The runtime consumer.** It is optional and off by default
(`TOON_ENABLE_AVATAR_STATE`). It needs `usd-avatar-runtime`'s experimental C
ABI revision 4, which is not frozen; a later revision needs a rebuild. The
provider hosts are opt-in (`TOON_AVATAR_RUNTIME_PROVIDERS`), never installed
and not in the package: `toon-hydra2-avatar-provider-test` and
`toon-viewport-avatar`. Core, backend and Hydra-only builds stay
independent of the consumer.

**Artifacts.** The release lane produces the `hydra` renderer package with
its manifest and SPDX SBOM, a source archive, checksums and archive/OCI
pins. It pushes
`ghcr.io/animu-sphere/hydra-toon:toon-0.3.0-cy2026-windows-x86_64-py313-lookdev`.
No artifact or published digest is claimed before the tagged workflow;
the release gate's local runs are
[renderer report 52](../reports/renderer/52-2026-10-11-v030-preparation.md). A
Formation is re-pinned and GPU-tested after publication.

## Known limitations

- **Parity.** The Hydra side is the session-layer oracle. Parity with the
  runtime's own Hydra publication waits for that publication.
- **Material binds.** They are established on the committed fixture only;
  no local avatar binds material colours.
- **Latency.** Expression and gaze input is generated, not captured; no
  connector's input was measured. The runtime has no live pose intake, and
  a clip pose has no production time, so pose latency is unmeasured. Present
  API return is not scanout, and no display time is measured.
- **Coverage.** Two local 128-joint avatars and seven clips, not every VRM.
  VRM springs and constraints are not evaluated.
- **Unchanged from v0.2.0.** Dual quaternion skinning keeps the CPU path, and
  a CPU-skinned mesh that Hydra keeps animating rewrites its points. Asset
  uploads stay on the render thread. Linux, macOS and AMD/Intel GPUs are
  unmeasured; MMD, generic PreviewSurface and WebGPU are later milestones.
